# Meeting Follow-Up Email Creator

## Purpose

Create a warm, customer-facing follow-up email from meeting notes, transcripts, call summaries, or rough meeting inputs.

Use this when the goal is to send a polished recap email after a customer meeting that strengthens the relationship, reinforces alignment, and clearly captures next steps without sounding robotic or overly task-focused.

---

## Core Goal

The email should feel like a thoughtful follow-up from a CSM, not just a list of action items.

It should:
- Make the customer feel heard
- Reinforce partnership and momentum
- Reflect the customer’s priorities in their own language
- Summarize the conversation clearly
- Include action items only where useful
- Keep the tone warm, human, and professional

---

## Output

Draft a customer-facing email.

### Required Elements

- **Subject line** — Clear, specific, and natural
- **Opening** — Warm thank-you that references the meeting naturally
- **Relationship-building note** — A short sentence or paragraph that acknowledges the customer’s context, goals, transition, priorities, or effort
- **Recap** — A concise summary of what was discussed, written in a conversational way
- **Next steps / action items** — Include only the most important next steps, clearly owned where needed
- **Closing** — Reinforce that the CSM is looking forward to partnering with them

---

## Important Style Guidance

Do not make every email follow the exact same structure.

Avoid defaulting to too many headings like:
- Agreed action items
- My action items
- Customer action items
- Next step

Use those sections only when the meeting was highly tactical or had several clear owners.

For most customer emails, prefer a smoother structure:
- Warm opening
- Short relationship-building paragraph
- Recap of key discussion themes
- Light next steps section
- Warm close

---

## Relationship-Building Requirements

Where relevant, include 1–2 sentences that help build trust and continuity.

Examples:
- “I appreciated the additional context around how your team is thinking about this internally.”
- “It was helpful to better understand where your team is focused this quarter.”
- “I’m excited to partner with you as we work through this next phase.”
- “I know there are a few moving pieces here, so my goal is to keep things clear and easy to manage from the 6sense side.”
- “It was great to hear how your team is thinking about scaling this beyond the current setup.”

Only include this if it fits naturally. Do not overdo it.

---

## Email Structure Options

Choose the structure based on the meeting type.

### 1. Introductory / Relationship-Building Meeting

Use this when the meeting was a first introduction, transition call, or light discovery.

**Subject:** Great connecting today

Hi [Customer Name],

Thank you again for taking the time to meet today. It was great to officially connect and learn more about your team’s priorities.

[Add a short relationship-building paragraph based on the customer context.]

A few things I took away from our conversation:
- [Theme 1]
- [Theme 2]
- [Theme 3]

From my side, I’ll [CSM-owned next step]. If there is anything else you would like me to prioritize, please feel free to send it my way.

Looking forward to working together.

Best,  
[Name]

---

### 2. Strategic Planning / Roadmap / Account Direction Meeting

Use this when the customer discussed priorities, roadmap, expansion, maturity, renewal, or broader planning.

**Subject:** Follow-up and next steps from today’s discussion

Hi [Customer Name],

Thank you again for the conversation today. I appreciated the context you shared around [customer priority / business initiative / transition].

It sounds like the main areas we want to focus on are [brief summary]. I’m excited to partner with you on this and make sure we keep the next steps practical and aligned to what matters most for your team.

A few key themes from today:
- [Theme 1]
- [Theme 2]
- [Theme 3]

As next steps:
- **6sense:** [CSM action]
- **Customer team:** [Customer action, if applicable]
- **Together:** [Shared next step, if applicable]

I’ll follow up with [specific next step / timing]. Looking forward to continuing the momentum.

Best,  
[Name]

---

### 3. Tactical Working Session / Product Enablement Meeting

Use this when the meeting focused on workflows, training, campaign setup, integrations, troubleshooting, reporting, or product usage.

**Subject:** Next steps from today’s working session

Hi [Customer Name],

Thanks again for the time today. It was helpful to walk through [topic] and align on where we can make the process smoother for the team.

Based on the discussion, the main focus areas are:
- [Focus area 1]
- [Focus area 2]
- [Focus area 3]

I’ll take the lead on [CSM-owned action]. From your side, the main item is [customer-owned action, if any].

Once that is in place, we can [next logical step].

Thanks again — looking forward to helping move this forward.

Best,  
[Name]

---

### 4. Support / Blocker / Issue Follow-Up

Use this when the meeting involved an issue, support ticket, technical blocker, or customer frustration.

**Subject:** Follow-up on [issue/topic]

Hi [Customer Name],

Thank you for walking me through the issue today. I understand this has created some friction, and I appreciate you sharing the details so we can get the right eyes on it.

Here is what I captured:
- [Issue detail 1]
- [Issue detail 2]
- [Issue detail 3]

From my side, I will [CSM action, such as raise a support ticket, follow up internally, check documentation, confirm expected behavior].

I’ll keep you posted as soon as I have an update. In the meantime, if anything changes or you notice additional details, please send them my way.

Best,  
[Name]

---

## Action Item Rules

Action items should be clear but not dominate the email.

Use action items when:
- Owners were clearly discussed
- The customer expects a written recap
- Multiple next steps exist
- There is a project, implementation, support issue, or renewal motion

Do not force separate “My action items” and “Customer action items” sections unless the meeting truly requires that level of structure.

Preferred format:

**Next steps**
- **6sense:** [Action]
- **Customer team:** [Action]
- **Together:** [Action]

If there is only one next step, write it naturally in paragraph form instead of using bullets.

---

## Tone

The tone should be:
- Warm
- Human
- Professional
- Customer-friendly
- Clear
- Confident
- Not overly formal
- Not robotic
- Not too long

The email should sound like it came from a thoughtful CSM who is building a long-term relationship.

---

## What Not To Include

Never include:
- Internal concerns
- Hidden risks not openly discussed with the customer
- Internal strategy
- Sensitive commercial positioning unless it was explicitly discussed
- Negative commentary about the customer
- Overly detailed transcript-style summaries
- Too many bullets that make the email feel like meeting minutes

---

## Final Check Before Output

Before finalizing the email, check:

1. Does the opening feel warm and specific?
2. Does the email reflect the customer’s context?
3. Does it build relationship, not just list tasks?
4. Are the next steps clear but not overwhelming?
5. Does it sound like a real CSM wrote it?
6. Is it concise enough for a customer to actually read?

If the draft feels too much like a project tracker, rewrite it to be more conversational.
